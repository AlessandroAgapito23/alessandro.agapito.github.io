Research

Standard sirens as probes of cosmology, astrophysics and fundamental physics

§ Overview

Gravitational waves from merging compact binaries give a self-calibrated measurement of the luminosity distance, but not of the redshift. Siren methods recover the missing redshift information from somewhere else: features in the source-frame mass distribution (spectral sirens) or catalogues of candidate host galaxies (dark sirens). Together they give an independent route to H0 and to the Hubble tension.

dL(z) ≈ cz / H0,    mdet = (1 + z) msrc The signal gives dL and the redshifted masses. A known scale in the source-frame masses, or a host galaxy, supplies z.

My Ph.D., supervised by Michele Mancarella at the Centre de Physique Théorique in Marseille, targets the systematics that limit these methods: the mass model and its redshift evolution, and the galaxy–merger relation. I design the methods to scale from the current LVK network to third-generation detectors, using hierarchical population models, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo on GPUs, and normalizing flows for expensive steps such as selection effects.

§ Current work

Current work

Spectral sirens

H0 is robust to redshift evolution of the black-hole mass spectrumlead author

We revisited spectral-siren constraints while letting the main scales of the binary-black-hole primary-mass spectrum evolve with redshift, the first such study at current sensitivity. I developed metrics based on the Kullback–Leibler divergence to assess evolution at each mass scale and parameter level. The constraints stay robust, with no compelling evidence for evolution; the evolving model shifts H0 slightly and non-significantly lower.

More

Event- and posterior-level diagnostics trace that low-H0 tail to high-mass features at higher redshift. O5-like simulations show how higher-redshift events will pin the spectral features directly from data.

Phys. Rev. D 114, 063503 (2026) · arXiv:2605.20112
Dark sirens

Measuring the galaxy–merger relation and ranking individual hostslead author

Dark-siren analyses usually marginalize over the unknown host and fix the probability that each galaxy hosts a merger. We invert that logic: the luminosity-dependent galaxy–merger relation and the probability of each candidate host are inferred jointly with cosmology and the binary population, accounting for both GW and galaxy selection effects.

More

A mixed discrete–continuous scheme, built on pymcpop-gw and compatible with HMC in PyMC, samples up to O(10³) parameters. Simulations recover the injected relations. Treating GW170817 as a dark siren, we find a mild preference for non-uniform weights and place the true host, NGC 4993, among the top-ranked candidates. Wrong weighting assumptions bias the per-event host ranking, which matters for electromagnetic follow-up.

arXiv:2609.38110 · submitted to PRL · pymcpop-gw
Population reconstruction

A weakly modelled view of the joint compact-binary mass planecorresponding author

With V. De Renzis, a JAX/NumPyro mixture-model reconstruction of the population in the joint source-frame mass plane, running HMC on GPUs. On GWTC-5, keeping the full two-dimensional mass distribution tightens cosmological constraints relative to a flexible one-dimensional model.

More

Alongside it, a model-independent way to identify population features and quantify their stability and prominence directly from the inferred density (posterior-landscape), tracking how each feature correlates with cosmology. Features carry information in two ways: coherent anchoring of a mass scale, and genuinely two-dimensional geometry that a primary-mass spectrum with a global mass-ratio prescription cannot represent.

Third generation & LVK

Forecasts for the Einstein Telescope and LVK production analyses

A blinded, GPU-accelerated mock data challenge pushing spectral-siren pipelines (chimera, icarogw, pymcpop-gw) to the 3G regime. In the LVK cosmology working group I run collaboration pipelines, including icarogw, on GWTC-6 data for the cosmology paper expected in December 2026.

More

In my M.Sc. thesis I extended the Fisher-matrix code GWFish to test the nature of compact objects and horizon-scale physics at ET sensitivity; the results are part of the ET Blue Book. I also contribute to ET design-study work with GWFAST.

§ Next

Open questions I want to work on

Q1 · dark sirens

Which galaxies host compact-binary mergers?

Map the trade-off between localization accuracy and galaxy–merger information, keep the cost down with GPU sampling, then move past a single universal host relation toward heterogeneous galaxy subpopulations inferred jointly with the luminosity function.

Q2 · spectral sirens

How flexible can a mass model be before it stops measuring H0?

Extend the weakly modelled reconstruction to the joint mass–redshift space and to spin, with explicit robustness tests on the priors and model construction that flexibility quietly reintroduces.

Q3 · cross-correlations

Do gravitational waves trace galaxies the way galaxies do?

Use the measured galaxy–merger relation to interpret the GW clustering bias and to test the common assumption that GW and galaxy biases coincide, ahead of IGWN IR1, Euclid DR1 and the Einstein Telescope.

A compact-binary chirp h(t): its amplitude fixes the luminosity distance dL. The redshift z has to come from somewhere else.
